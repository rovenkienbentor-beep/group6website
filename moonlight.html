Chapter 1: Bleachers and Bruised Egos

Transferring to Moonlight High for the first day of senior year was supposed to be an exercise in invisibility for Julian. Back in Oakridge, his name had been printed on the back of varsity jerseys and plastered across local sports columns—until a wet turf field, a late tackle, and a torn meniscus had ended his scholarship prospects in under three seconds. He had spent the summer learning how to walk without a limp and how to ignore the pitying stares of neighbors who used to ask him for autographs. Moving two towns over was meant to be a clean slate: no expectations, no spotlight, no baggage.

Standing at the edge of Moonlight High’s sprawling courtyard under a hazy September sun, Julian adjusted the strap of his canvas backpack and watched groups of students reunite beside rows of dented lockers. He kept his head down, heading toward the administrative office to pick up his schedule.

He made it ten paces before someone darted out from behind a brick pillar, colliding squarely with his bad shoulder.

Books, loose-leaf binder paper, and a half-empty iced coffee exploded across the concrete. Julian winced, catching his balance against the wall just as the other person scrambled backward onto their heels.

"I am so sorry, I wasn't looking—watch the coffee—"

The voice stopped dead.

Julian blinked against the glare of the sun, looking down at a girl kneeling amidst a sea of ruined notes. She wore a vintage denim jacket covered in enamel pins, her dark hair pulled back into a messy bun held together by a yellow pencil. When she looked up, her wide hazel eyes locked onto his, and the color drained entirely from her face.

"Julian?" Maya breathed, the word barely audible over the chatter of the courtyard.

Julian’s stomach dropped straight through his sneakers. He hadn't seen Maya since the summer before freshman year—right before his dad took a coaching job upstate and Julian had promised he’d call every single weekend, a promise he had broken within two months as his new life took over. She wasn't the awkward kid with oversized braces who used to trade comic books with him on the curb anymore. She looked sharp, composed, and entirely horrified to see him standing there.

"Hey, Maya," Julian said, his voice cracking slightly as he knelt down to gather her scattered papers, suddenly hyper-aware of the faint click in his knee. "Long time no see."
