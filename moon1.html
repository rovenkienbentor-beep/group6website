Chapter 2: Locked in the Science Lab

For the first two weeks of school, they perfected the art of polite avoidance. Maya was the editor-in-chief of The Lunar Chronicle, Moonlight High’s surprisingly cutthroat student newspaper, and she moved through the hallways with the brisk efficiency of someone perpetually five minutes behind a deadline. Julian kept to the back rows of his classes, doodling architectural sketches in the margins of his notebooks and eating lunch alone on the far side of the football bleachers, watching the varsity team run drills without him.

Their truce shattered on a rainy Tuesday afternoon in AP Chemistry.

Mr. Harrison, a teacher who believed deeply in the character-building power of random pairing, tapped his clipboard twice. "Maya Lin and Julian Vance. Station four. You’re analyzing reaction kinetics for the midterm capstone. If you don't have your titration curves logged by Friday, don't bother showing up to my Monday lecture."

Maya’s pen snapped against her desk. Julian stared intensely at his shoelaces.

By five o'clock that evening, the rest of the students had cleared out, leaving the third-floor science wing dead silent save for the steady drumming of rain against the tall windows. Maya stood at the lab bench with her arms crossed tightly over her chest, watching Julian carefully adjust the burette valve drop by drop.

"You didn't even send a text," Maya said suddenly, her voice flat and sharp in the quiet room.

Julian’s hand twitched, nearly ruining the phenolphthalein indicator. He set the glass stirring rod down slowly and exhaled, leaning his palms against the black slate counter. "I know, Maya. I messed up."

"You disappeared," she corrected, stepping closer, her hazel eyes flashing with four years of banked frustration. "My mom had that awful surgery that winter, and I kept waiting for you to call because you were my best friend, Julian. Then I saw your photo in the state sports section smiling with your new team, and I realized you just... upgraded."

The words hit harder than any tackle ever had. Julian looked at her—really looked at her—and saw the genuine hurt still sitting just beneath her tough, editor-in-chief armor. "I didn't upgrade," he said quietly, meeting her gaze without flinching. "I was fourteen, overwhelmed, and terrified that if I called you, I’d realize how much I hated leaving. Every week I waited, it got harder to pick up the phone until I felt like a total coward. And then... well. Karma caught up with me pretty fast."

Maya’s gaze dropped involuntarily toward his right knee before flicking back up to his face. The anger in her shoulders visibly deflated, replaced by a complicated, hesitant stillness. "You still draw little spaceships in the margins of your chemistry notes," she murmured, gesturing toward his open spiral notebook.

Julian felt a faint, crooked smile tug at the corner of his mouth. "Only when the lecture gets boring. You still chew on the erasers of your pencils?"

Maya glanced down at the mangled pink nub in her hand and let out a reluctant, breathless laugh. "Shut up and read the meniscus, Vance. We still have three trials left."
