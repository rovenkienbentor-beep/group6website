Chapter 3: The Hidden Room

That evening, they met at the old library. The building was nearly empty. He led her toward the oldest section, where shelves covered the walls from floor to ceiling.


“My grandmother said there was something hidden here,” he whispered.

“Your grandmother told you this?”

“Not exactly.”


He pulled out an old key.


“My father gave me this before he disappeared.”


She stared at the key. “You never told me your father disappeared.”

“There are a lot of things I never told you.”


She wanted to ask why. Instead, she followed him.


They reached the final shelf. He inserted the key into a small lock hidden beneath the wooden frame.


Click.


The entire bookshelf moved.


Behind it was a narrow staircase.


She stared in disbelief. “You have got to be kidding me.”

“I wish I were.”


They descended. At the bottom was a small room filled with old books, photographs, and newspaper clippings.


She picked up one of the photographs. It showed the city decades ago.


And standing in the center were the same two people from the photograph.


“They haven't aged,” she whispered.

He looked over her shoulder. “I know.”


Suddenly, they heard footsteps above them.


Both froze.


“Did you tell anyone we were coming?” she whispered.

“No.”


The footsteps grew louder.


He grabbed her hand. “Come on.”


They hid behind one of the shelves.


The door opened.


A shadow appeared at the bottom of the stairs.


Then a voice spoke.


“You shouldn't have opened that room.”


She recognized the voice.


Her heart dropped.


It belonged to someone she trusted.
