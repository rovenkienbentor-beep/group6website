Chapter 3: Under the Friday Night Lights

Once the ice broke, the old rhythm between them returned with startling speed. Chemistry lab sessions bled into two-hour marathons at the booth in the back of Starlight Diner, where Maya edited layout proofs on her laptop while Julian sketched designs for a community rec center—a new dream he had kept secret from everyone, including his parents.

Maya was the first person who didn't look at his knee brace like it was a tragedy. Instead, she pushed his sketchbook across the table one night, tapping a finger against his rough draft of a vaulted skylight. "Your proportions here are way off, Julian, the load-bearing beams wouldn't survive a heavy snow. Here, look—" She pulled up architectural blueprints on her tablet, her eyes lighting up with the same fierce enthusiasm she used to have when they built treehouses out of scrap lumber.

In return, Julian became her quiet anchor during the chaotic weeks leading up to the Homecoming issue. When the school administration threatened to pull funding from The Lunar Chronicle over Maya’s investigative piece on broken heating systems in the arts wing, it was Julian who stayed up until midnight helping her cross-reference maintenance budgets and photocopy receipts.

On the night of the Homecoming bonfire, the entire student body gathered in the gravel lot behind the stadium. Sparks spiraled up into the crisp October sky while the pep band played off-key pop songs. Maya stood near the edge of the crowd, shivering slightly in a thin cardigan as she snapped photos for the paper.

Julian walked up behind her and draped his heavy varsity letterman jacket—the patches stripped off the sleeves—over her shoulders. Maya looked up in surprise as he pulled her gently away from the roar of the crowd toward the quiet shadow of the empty bleachers.

"You’re missing the big rally," she teased softly, pulling the oversized lapels closed around herself.

"Never liked crowds much anyway," Julian said, leaning back against the metal railing. He looked down at her, the orange glow of the distant fire catching the gold flecks in her eyes. "Maya... when my dad told me we were moving back to this district, I thought my life was officially over. Turns out, it was the only good break I've gotten all year."

Maya’s breath caught, her fingers tightening on the wool of his jacket. For a second, neither of them moved, the noise of the bonfire fading into a distant hum.
