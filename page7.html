Chapter 5: The Choice

They spent the night in an abandoned observatory outside the city. Neither of them slept.

She sat beside the window, watching the red moon slowly disappear behind the clouds.

“So what happens now?” she asked.

He remained silent.


“You can't keep protecting me without telling me the truth.”


“I know.”


“Then tell me.”


He sat beside her.

“My father believed that the Red Moon Society was responsible for a series of disappearances.”


“And?”

“He discovered that they were searching for someone.”


“Me.”


He nodded.

“But why?”

“I don't know.”


She looked down at her hands.

For some reason, the strange symbol from the street was still faintly visible on her palm.

She touched it.

It began to glow.

He immediately stood.


“What?”

“The mark.”

“What about it?”

“It wasn't there before.”

She looked at him.


“What does it mean?”

Before he could answer, glass shattered behind them.

They turned.

A figure stood on the other side of the window, wearing a dark coat and watching them.

He pulled her behind him.


“Stay here.”


The figure disappeared.

They rushed outside.

Nothing.


Only the cold night air.

Then she noticed something lying on the ground.


A letter.


Her name was written across the front.

She opened it with shaking hands.

There were only three sentences.


You have been hiding from us for eighteen years.

You were never meant to survive.

And he was never meant to love you.


She slowly lowered the letter.

He read it over her shoulder.

For once, he had no answer.

She looked at him.


“Did you know?”

“No.”

“Are you sure?”

“Yes.”


She studied his face.

Then she remembered everything—every argument, every time he had protected her, and every moment when he could have walked away but didn't.

She stepped closer.


“I don't know who I can trust anymore.”

He looked at her.

“You can trust me.”

“You're the person I'm supposed to stay away from.”

“I know.”

“You're supposed to hate me.”

“I know.”

“Then why don't you?”


He didn't answer immediately.

Instead, he reached for her hand.


“Because somewhere along the way,” he whispered, “you stopped being the person I was supposed to protect.”


She looked into his eyes.

“What did I become?”


His voice softened.

“Someone I can't lose.”


The red moon disappeared behind the clouds.

And somewhere in the distance, a bell rang.


Once.


Twice.


Three times.


They both looked toward the city.

A hundred lights suddenly turned on at the same time.

Then the symbol on her palm burned brighter than ever.

The Red Moon Society had found them.

And this time, they were not running.
