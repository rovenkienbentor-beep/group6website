Chapter 3: Glitching Through Time

Without voice chat, relying purely on text macros and muscle memory built a decade ago, they ran down the dirt road toward the Sunken Crypt—the very first five-man dungeon in the game. Leo unequipped his legendary raid bow, switching to a rusted iron shortbow he kept in his bank vault for sentimental reasons so he wouldn't one-shot the monsters.

It was clumsy, slow, and absurdly fun. They mistimed crowd-control spells, accidentally pulled three groups of skeletal warriors at once, and nearly wiped on a mini-boss that Leo used to solo in his sleep. Every time one of them narrowly survived with a sliver of health, the chat box filled with keyboard-smash laughter and frantic emotes.

[Party] Lyra_Mist: I forgot how terrible the pathfinding in this hallway was.

[Party] Kaelen_V: It's not a bug, it's atmospheric tension. Watch the spike trap on your left.

By the time they defeated the crypt’s final boss—a giant, clunky stone necromancer—the system broadcast banner flashed crimson across the top of their screens:

[SYSTEM] ATTENTION: Server shutdown in 00:03:00. Thank you for adventuring in the Realm of Ashes.

They climbed out of the crypt onto the grassy cliffs overlooking the pixelated ocean just as the skybox shifted to midnight. Leo waited for the inevitable freeze—the sudden rubber-banding lag followed by the cold Disconnected from Server pop-up.

The countdown hit 00:00:01.

And stayed there.

The ambient ocean waves kept lapping against the shore. The stars in the purple skybox continued to twinkle. Confused, Leo checked his network monitor—packets were still flowing, though the global chat channel had gone completely dead.

[Party] Lyra_Mist: ...Uh. Did your game crash too, or are we ghosts?

[Party] Kaelen_V: Looks like the shutdown script hung on our instance. Guess the janitor forgot to unplug the blade server.

With the dread of the deadline suddenly suspended in digital limbo, neither of them logged out. They sat their characters side-by-side on the edge of the cliff, watching the looped digital horizon, and began to talk—not about patch notes or raid mechanics, but about the real lives they had built while the game was slowly dying.

Leo learned that "Lyra" was Clara, a twenty-seven-year-old architectural restorer living in Chicago who used to play Realm of Ashes with her older brother before he moved abroad. Clara learned that Leo was a twenty-eight-year-old sound designer in Seattle who had met his closest friends through this exact starting zone when he was nineteen and terrified of college.

[Party] Lyra_Mist: It’s weird, isn't it? Nine years of memories stored on a hard drive in California, and tomorrow morning someone formats it and it’s like none of us were ever here.

Leo stared at the glowing blue text, a sudden, sharp ache settling behind his ribs. For the first time in years, he didn't want to hit the exit button.
