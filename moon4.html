Chapter 5: The Final Bell

For three weeks, the silence between them was worse than the four years they had spent apart. Maya buried herself in layout deadlines, declining his calls and sitting on the opposite side of the chemistry room. Julian didn't push; he realized words had already failed him once before. If he wanted a second chance, he had to build something real enough to prove he wasn't going anywhere.

The night of the Winter Formal arrived in a flurry of early December snow. Maya stood near the entrance of the decorated gymnasium, checking tickets at the welcome table in a dark emerald dress, trying very hard not to look toward the front doors every time the cold wind blew in.

At eight o'clock sharp, the double doors swung open.

Julian walked in wearing a tailored charcoal suit—no brace visible beneath the trousers, though he still favored his left side slightly—carrying a large, flat architectural presentation board wrapped in brown butcher paper. He walked straight past the photo booth and stopped directly in front of Maya’s table.

"You're late," Maya said quietly, gripping her clipboard like a shield. "And you didn't buy a ticket."

"I brought an admission fee instead," Julian said softly, setting the board down on the table and tearing away the paper.

It wasn't a cheesy prom proposal poster. It was a fully rendered, ink-and-watercolor architectural blueprint of a modern community library and journalism center—complete with a vaulted glass skylight, a darkroom in the basement, and a tiny, familiar doodle of a spaceship tucked neatly into the corner margin. Printed in neat block letters along the title block was: The Lin-Vance Media & Design Wing — Submitted for the State Student Architecture Competition.

Maya stared at the drawing, her breath hitching as a single tear spilled over her lashes onto the crisp white vellum. "Julian..."

"I submitted my portfolio to the state design program yesterday," he said, his voice steady and warm as he reached across the table, offering his hand palm-up. "No football. No Oakridge. Just four years of architecture school thirty minutes down the road from the state journalism college you applied to. I'm not going anywhere, Maya—unless you want me to."

Maya looked at the blueprint, then up into the earnest, open eyes of the boy who had finally learned how to stay. Letting out a watery laugh, she set her clipboard aside and slipped her hand into his.

"Come on, Vance," she whispered, pulling him toward the dance floor beneath the canopy of fairy lights. "You owe me a dance."
